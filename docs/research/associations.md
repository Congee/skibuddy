# Association drill catalogue

Research for association mappings. Checked 2026-10-15. Every URL below returned HTTP 200 to `curl` with a browser user agent on that date, except where marked. Descriptions are paraphrased; only short fundamentals labels are quoted.

skibuddy is association-neutral: a drill has a generic name, and each association's task is a mapping with the task name, the level on that association's own scale, an optional category, its fundamentals and a URL. This file records what each association publishes so those mappings can be written and checked.

## PSIA-AASI (United States)

### Levels and fundamentals

Three certification levels: Level 1, Level 2, Level 3. The Alpine Certification Standards list five **Alpine Skiing Fundamentals**. Paraphrased here, ids as in `content/fundamentals/psia.yaml`; the wording is PSIA's, in the [Standards PDF](https://thesnowpros.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/PSIA_Alpine_Standards100225v5.pdf):

1. `fore-aft`: fore-aft balance over the feet, with pressure along the ski.
2. `ski-to-ski`: pressure between the skis, mostly to the outside ski.
3. `edge-angles`: edge angle from leaning in and angulating.
4. `leg-rotation`: turning the skis with the legs, under a quiet upper body.
5. `pressure-magnitude`: how much pressure builds between ski and snow.

The 2025 PDF's wording of the first differs slightly from PSIA-C's pages, which looks like a layout slip.

Activities fall into three **categories**: Integrated, Individual and Versatility. The word "tasks" has been replaced by "assessment activities".

### Other divisions

- **Rocky Mountain** ([IDP Skiing Standards](https://www.psia-rm.org/wp-content/uploads/IDP-Skiing-Standards-1.pdf), undated, older) groups tasks by skill pairs (Rotary/Pressure Control, Edging/Pressure Control). ## CSIA (Canadian Ski Instructors' Alliance)

### Levels and skills

Level 1 to Level 4. The current framework is the **5 Skills Framework**: Balance, Rotary, Edging, Pressure, Coordination ([The 5 Skills Framework](https://csia.snowpro.com/ServicesServlet/telechargement/document/The5SkillsFramework.pdf); also in the [Canadian Teaching Approach](https://csia.snowpro.com/ServicesServlet/telechargement/document/CTA_EN.pdf), updated Sept 2025). An older five-skill list (stance and balance, pivoting, edging, pressure control, timing and coordination) still appears in third-party material; do not use it for new mappings. The framework also classifies ski performance as skidded, steered or carved.

### Named tasks

CSIA publishes **ski-off runs and tactic runs**, not a drill catalogue. Each one has a speed, a performance type (skidded/steered/carved), a pitch and a surface.

| 2 | ski-off run | Short Turn (less than 1 cat track) | same |

Pitfalls:
- CSIA's **"Edge to Edge (Hop Turn)"** is a *carved* turn with a small hop from outside ski to outside ski at transition. It is not the pivoted Spiess-style hop turn that PSIA and NZSIA mean. It is closer to PSIA's "Short turn Leapers" / PSIA-C "Dynamic Leapers".
- **Braquage** is a skidded fall-line rotation of parallel skis with no arc. It is the nearest CSIA match for pivot slips, but CSIA does not call it that.

Drill words in learner tactics: [Getting Started](https://csia.snowpro.com/ServicesServlet/telechargement/document/Getting_Started.pdf) lists side slipping, hockey stops, hops, one-ski sliding and airplane turns as tactics for children; the [L1 Candidate Guide](https://csia.snowpro.com/ServicesServlet/telechargement/document/L1CG-EN.pdf) mentions one-ski turns. None of them has a level or category.

### Public vs. member-only

Public: candidate guides L1–L3, the Skills Framework, the Canadian Teaching Approach and Getting Started. No Level 4 guide was found at the same URL pattern. The CSIA Technical Reference and member videos are on snowpro.com behind a login (not tested).

### Copyright

Getting Started says "All rights reserved – Canadian Ski Instructors' Alliance". No open licence. Link and paraphrase.

## BASI (British Association of Snowsport Instructors)

### Levels and framework

Level 1, Level 2, Level 3 (ISIA), Level 4 (ISTD). BASI's model has three layers:

- **Fundamental Elements** (verbatim, [basi.study pre-course page](https://www.basi.study/course/fundamental-elements)): Body Management; Steering Elements; Control of Speed & Line. BASI's Level 3/4 PIA charts use the same headings: control of line, control of speed, steering elements, body management.
- **Central Theme** ([basi.study](https://www.basi.study/course/central-theme)): the beginner-to-parallel progression. The stage names on BASI's own pages are plough turn, plough parallel and parallel. Forum accounts of the full stage list (straight running, plough glide, three plough-turn stages, three plough-parallel stages, basic parallel, parallel with poles) were not confirmed on an official page.
- **Performance Threads**: Equipment, Environment, Physical, Psychological, Tactical, Technical ([Level 3/4 PIA charts](https://www.basiinterski.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Level-34-PIAs.pdf); the [BASI Interski article](https://www.basiinterski.org.uk/the-world-interski-congress-and-basis-national-education-team-why-are-they-important/) describes the history). The skiing strands in the PIA charts are Long Turns, Short Turns, Bumps, Variables and Steeps (all labelled Level 3/4).

### Named tasks

No official public page names BASI drills such as javelins, storks, pivot slips or railroad tracks. Course-provider blogs (e.g. skinewgen.com) mention them, but those are not BASI sources. Officially public names are limited to the Central Theme stages and the PIA strands:

## APSI (Australian Professional Snowsport Instructors)

### Levels and framework

Alpine Level 1 to Level 4 ([apsi.net.au](https://www.apsi.net.au/)). APSI's course pages describe the teaching system as built around the performance of **steering**: [Level 2](https://www.apsi.net.au/courses-events/leveltwo/alpine) goes up to steered parallel turns, [Level 3](https://www.apsi.net.au/courses-events/levelthree/alpine) up to carved medium turns and short turns, and [Level 4](https://www.apsi.net.au/courses-events/levelfour/alpine) up to pure carved turns and racing. Exams are station-based, each task skied twice with the best mark counting. Level 3 components: Freeskiing, Demonstrations, Teaching, Movement Analysis, Theory.

APSI's skills names were found only in secondary sources: a [PSIA-W Interski 2023 report](https://psia-w.org/interski-2023/) describes the Australian model as four skills (rotary, edging, pressure, stance) mixed in different proportions, and a BASI report on an Interski adaptive workshop describes Steering, Carving and Pure Carving built from Stance, Rotary, Edging and Pressure. **Neither is an APSI publication.** Verify against the APSI Teaching Manual before using these labels.

### Named tasks

None found publicly. The course pages name assessment components, not drills.

### Public vs. member-only

The APSI Teaching Manual is included with membership (digital access); see [Manuals, Certificates, Pins](https://www.apsi.net.au/manuals-certificates-pins). The APSI Snowsports App (drills and videos) is mentioned in the BASI Interski report and was not checked.

### Copyright

No public licence. Everything that matters sits behind membership.

## IASI (Irish Association of Snowsports Instructors)

Not to be confused with iasi.ie, which belongs to an unrelated accommodation body. The association's site is [iasisnowsports.ie](https://iasisnowsports.ie/).

### Levels and framework

Alpine has four levels ([Alpine page](https://iasisnowsports.ie/education/alpine/)):

- **Level 1 Ski Instructor:** closed or controlled areas such as artificial slopes, indoor domes and beginner areas. A Level 1 Rolling Carpet award is an alternative route.
- **Level 2 Ski Instructor:** on-piste work for a resort ski school.
- **Level 3 Ski Teacher:** carries the ISIA and IVSI stamps.
- **Level 4 Ski Teacher:** the top award, with outcomes of Euro Ski Pro (EU Common Technical Test) or the ISIA card.

From Level 2 up, IASI recommends skis of at least 14 m radius for women and 16 m for men.

The manual, *The Five Key Concepts* (2021), is organised around five models: the Basic Principles, the IASI Skills Model, Core Skier Development (the beginner-to-parallel progression), the Diamond Model of Skill Acquisition and the Pear Model. The Skills Model has four skills, rotation, edging, pressure control and timing, which sit inside two overriding fundamentals, balance and movements. IASI splits a turn into four phases: initiation, fall line, completion and transition.

### Named tasks

- **Drills:** the manual's drills chapter (ch. 17, [pp. 167–176](https://iasisnowsports.ie/mp-files/iasi-full-manual.pdf/#page=167)) is short and gives no levels. Exact names: Straight Running on one leg, Edged Garlands, Edged J Turns (p. 167); Side Slipping/edge set, Diagonal side slipping (p. 168); Javelin Turns (p. 169); Braquage (p. 170); Hop Turns/Speiss (p. 171); TicTacs (pp. 172–173); Dolphin Turns (p. 174); White Pass Turn (pp. 175–176). The only level stated anywhere is for Dolphin Turns, a Level 4 additional activity (p. 33). There is no stork turn.
- **Additional activities:** the manual calls drills that are assessed on the instructor pathway "additional activities". Ch. 4 (p. 48) lists traversing, side-slipping, falling leaf side slip, garlands and swing to the hill, with no levels. Ch. 17 does not describe them, despite saying it does.
- **Core Skier Development stages** (ch. 4, pp. 42–47): Straight running, Ploughing, Plough turning, Plough parallel, Basic parallel, Parallel using poles. The model comes from Tate's *Parallel Dreams Alpine Skiing*.
- **Piste turn forms** (ch. 6, pp. 58–72): Carved turns; Short turns (regular/steered, narrow corridor/quick, wide corridor/highly deflected); Parallel Variations (Intermediate Parallel, Advanced Parallel). The manual says these turn forms are part of the assessment criteria at different levels (p. 56), and that Parallel Variations are in the Level 3 and Level 4 outcome standards (p. 24).
- **Assessment criteria:** the per-level criteria downloads on the Alpine page (zips of PDFs and images, Nov 2019) name assessed run types, not drills. At Level 4 these are advanced parallel, short, medium/long and mixed turns (funnel, hourglass, rhythm changes), bumps and variable conditions. The Level 1–3 teaching criteria require Core Skier Development lessons and the ability to explain the why, what and how of each drill chosen.

### Suggested generic names

Use the plain English name that most associations already use, and avoid any one association's spelling or numerals. Where associations disagree:

- **thousand-steps**, not "1000 Steps": spell the number out.
- **javelin-turns**: PSIA says "Javelin", NZSIA "Javelin turns". Keep **reverse-javelin-turns** as a separate drill.
- **outside-ski-turns**, **whitepass-turns** and **one-ski-turns** are three different drills. PSIA's outside ski turn changes the stance ski at each transition; whitepass makes the transition on the old outside ski; NZSIA's "One ski" keeps the same ski for the whole run. Do not merge them. **outside-ski-j-turn** can be a variant of outside-ski-turns.
- **pivot-slips**: map PSIA Pivot Slips, RM/NZSIA "Linked pivot slips", and CSIA Braquage with a note that it is the nearest match. "Linked hockey slips" (RM) belongs under hockey-stops as a variant.
- **sideslips**, with variants for fall line, diagonal and edge set. PSIA spells it "Sideslip"; NZSIA "Side Slips". Prefer one word, as PSIA does.
- **hop-turns** for the pivoted, Spiess-style drill. **leaper-turns** for hops between carved or parallel turns. Map CSIA "Edge to Edge (Hop Turn)" to **leaper-turns**, not hop-turns, despite its name.
- **railroad-track-turns** (PSIA's full name; NZSIA says "Railroad tracks"). Variants: one-footed, garland.
- **garlands**: a generic drill with no current national task. Map PSIA-C's former tasks and mark them as such.
- **short-radius-turns**, **medium-radius-turns**, **long-radius-turns**. Add a "dynamic" variant for PSIA L3 / NZSIA. Prefer "long" to PSIA's "large", because BASI, Snowsport Austria (lange Radien) and SAJ (大回り) all say long.

## Implications for the mapping schema

These are observations only; content/ and src/ were not changed.

- **Level is not always a number.** NZSIA has "Trainer"; BASI's PIA strands say "Level 3/4"; SAJ grades run downwards (5級 → 1級 → テクニカル → クラウン); Snowsport Austria's stages are colours for learners. A string label with an optional ordinal works for all of them.
- **Category** values: PSIA Integrated/Individual/Versatility; CSIA ski-off run/tactic run; NZSIA's movement groups (Rotational/Lateral/Timing and coordination); BASI strands.
- **Fundamentals**: only PSIA-C publishes them per task. Elsewhere, fundamentals in a mapping are our inference from the association's framework, so mark them that way, as the activation evidence is marked.
- **URL**: the PSIA national list is a single PDF; `#page=N` deep-links each activity.
- The existing `content/associations/psia/stork-turns.yaml` uses the PSIA-C page and the PSIA-C Fundamental Focus. Both match what the page showed on 2026-10-15. The page joins the two fundamentals with "and/or", which a list does not capture.
